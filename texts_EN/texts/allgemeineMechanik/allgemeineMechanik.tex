\begin{section}{Energy}
\begin{subsection}{Potential Energy of a System of Point Masses with Gravitation}
So far, the concept of energy has not appeared. We consider the equations of motion of Newtonian gravitation:
\begin{eqnarray*}
F_k &=& \sum_{l\ne k} -n_{kl}\frac{Gm_km_l}{|x_k-x_l|^2}\\
m_k\ddot{x}_k &=& F_k
\end{eqnarray*}
$F_k$ is the force that gravitation exerts on point mass $k$ with mass $m_k$ and position $x_k$.
$n_{kl}=(x_k-x_l)/|x_k-x_l|$ is the unit vector from $x_l$ to $x_k$.
Work to move $k$ from $y_1$ to $y_2$ along $\gamma$ is path-independent:
$I_l(y_1,y_2)=1/|y_1-x_l|-1/|y_2-x_l|$, $U_k(x_k,y)=Gm_k\sum m_l(1/|x_k-x_l|-1/|y-x_l|)$.
For small $\delta x_k$, $U_k(x_k,x_k+\delta x_k)=-F_k\cdot\delta x_k$.
Potential energy choosing reference at infinity: $U_k(y)=-Gm_k\sum m_l/|y-x_l|$, $V_{kl}=-Gm_km_l/|x_k-x_l|$.
Total potential $V=\sum_{k<l}V_{kl}$ satisfies $\partial V/\partial x_n=-F_n$.
\end{subsection}
\begin{subsection}{Potential Energy of General Systems with Pair Potentials}
Generalization to point masses $m_k$ with pair potentials $V_{kl}(x_k,x_l)$. Example: electrically charged particles.
\end{subsection}
\begin{subsection}{Kinetic Energy}
$T=\frac12\sum m_k\dot{x}_k^2$, total energy $E=T+V$ conserved.
\end{subsection}
\end{section}
